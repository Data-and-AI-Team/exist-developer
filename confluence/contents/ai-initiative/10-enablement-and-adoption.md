# AI Enablement and Adoption

**Status:** Approved
**Owner:** Developer enablement owner / initiative sponsor
**Review cadence:** Quarterly and after major tool/policy changes  
**Approval and effective date:** November 15, 2025

## Purpose

Help teams use approved AI capabilities effectively, understand their boundaries, and demonstrate useful outcomes without relying on unverified productivity claims.

## Role-based learning

| Audience | Learning outcomes |
| --- | --- |
| All users | Approved tools, data classification, output verification, reporting concerns |
| Engineers | Coding-assistant workflow, review/testing, tool scopes, prompt injection |
| Product/delivery owners | Use-case selection, acceptance criteria, human oversight, benefit measurement |
| Technical/operations owners | Evaluation, deployment, monitoring, cost controls, incident response |
| Reviewers | Risk/data review, independent validation, approval and exception evidence |

## Pilot onboarding

1. Identify a sponsor, accountable owner, participants, approved tools/data, support channel, and bounded duration.
2. Capture the existing workflow baseline: completion time, quality, rework, review effort, and relevant user outcomes.
3. Provide task-specific training with authorized examples. Demonstrate failure cases, verification, escalation, and prohibited actions—not just successful prompts.
4. Confirm participants can perform the intended workflow, identify unsafe data use, and obtain help. Record completion without collecting unnecessary employee data.
5. Run the pilot with clear limits and feedback collection. Monitor quality, operational cost, and unexpected workload shifted to reviewers.
6. Compare outcomes with baseline, note uncertainty and selection bias, and decide whether to expand, adjust, or stop.

## Knowledge sharing and support

Maintain reviewed examples, short how-to guides, known limitations, and an owner-monitored support channel. Sanitize reusable examples. Route suspected disclosure or unauthorized actions immediately through incident handling, not a general discussion forum. Feed recurring questions and failures into training, tooling, and evaluation improvements.

## Adoption measures

Use voluntary/relevant feedback and proportionate aggregate reporting where possible. Measure successful task outcomes, review/rework burden, defect rates, satisfaction, time saved, and cost. Separate access provision, active use, and demonstrated benefit. Avoid equating prompt volume or generated code with performance, and explain any personnel-related monitoring before collecting it under applicable policy.

## Completion and evidence

Retain learning materials, role coverage, pilot scope/approval, baseline and outcome summaries, user feedback themes, and scale/stop decisions. Revisit training after provider changes, new permissions, policy changes, or incidents. Do not label participants trained or a pilot successful without supporting records.
